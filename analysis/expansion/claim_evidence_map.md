## Three different structural objects

1. **Simultaneous compatibility:** a graph H imposes all listed nucleotide-pair identities as constraints on the same genotype. A nucleotide may have several graph neighbors. Such a constraint graph need not be a physically realizable single RNA conformation. The theorem classifies synonymous sequence reconfiguration under those logical obligations.
2. **Alternative conformations:** separate target matchings S and T define separate compatible sets. Their intersection consists of genotypes individually compatible with each target. This is not evidence that both folds coexist, that every target is populated, or that the sequence can switch on a measured timescale. A union H answers intersection compatibility only. A native switch requires independently supported conformers and appropriate occupancy/context evidence.
3. **Ensemble folding:** the pair-retention score averages marginal pair probabilities in a declared folding ensemble at a fixed genotype. It is neither the probability of the whole target structure nor a physical transition rate. A union of marginally possible pairs is not an occupied joint fold. Thermodynamic superlevel connectivity is a model outcome, not a fitness landscape inferred from experiment.

## Interpretation ladder for controls

Uncorrelated score permutations preserve the score multiset and every threshold's component-specific retained counts but deliberately remove local score correlation. Degree conditioning additionally removes a simple graph-position explanation. Exhaustive small-space roughness conditioning retains the score multiset and one local-correlation statistic; its inference covers only its declared small-space domain. Heat-rank fields preserve the multiset exactly and fit expected roughness as a generic correlated reference. They are neither uniform conditional permutations nor a complete model of epistasis. Any residual contrast must survive fit diagnostics and cannot by itself be attributed to natural selection.

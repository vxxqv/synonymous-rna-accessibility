**Post-outcome analytical limitation of both classification baselines:** on the scored diagonal the withheld corner is an intermediate, so its two adjacent corners are the fixed endpoints. Their coordinatewise mean is at least their coordinatewise minimum. The adjacent-mean replacement is therefore jointly acceptable and opens a joint path. For the three-corner mean, write the known intermediate as z, the endpoints as s and t, and the replacement as c=(s+t+z)/3. Whenever z passes a trait threshold tau=min(s,t), c passes it too. Thus, for each trait, if either intermediate permits an individual path, c passes that trait: if z passes then so does c, and otherwise the individual path requires c to pass. If all traits individually have paths, c therefore gives a joint path. If some individual path fails, the classification is not joint-only either. Both baselines necessarily predict no joint-only obstruction under this frozen square construction. Their 0.5 balanced accuracy when both classes occur is not evidence of competitive learned classifiers. These facts do not invalidate the continuous MAE comparisons, whose frozen all-trait hurdles additive fails. Preserve the benchmark and disclose this post-outcome structural limitation; do not redesign it after these outcomes. The argument was checked independently here after a theorem-reviewer notification; the separate generalized lemma and fixtures are outside this review's numeric-verification scope.
